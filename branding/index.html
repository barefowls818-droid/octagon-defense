Octagon Defense: five brand directions

Each direction is a complete starting system with a logo, palette, type and voice, shown on a sample homepage. Pick one, or mix parts (for example, the logo from 4 with the colors from 1).

Where the brand is now

The flyer and the card look like two different companies. The flyer has the real asset: a black octagon with an outlined wordmark. The card swaps it for a shield, a world map and a swoosh.

  • Keep: red and black, the octagon, "Contract Protection" as the descriptor.
  • Drop: the shield (it says "security guard", the word he avoids), the world map (the work is Texas), and the fist (wrong signal for a de-escalation business).
  • One mark everywhere: whichever direction wins becomes the flyer, card, site, shirts and Google profile photo.

Why it works

It keeps what people in Lubbock may already recognize from the flyer, so the change reads as an upgrade, not a new company. Poster energy suits parties and venues.

Watch out for

The outlined wordmark needs size. Below about 120px wide it turns to noise, so the small mark drops the words. White text on this red is for big type only (4.4:1); small text on red goes black.
